Where Is Gallbladder Pain Felt? Front, Back, Shoulder and Left Side Explained

By The GallbladderPain.org editorial teamUpdated 3 November 2026

You've got a pain under your ribs and you're wondering: is that my gallbladder? This guide shows you exactly where gallbladder pain is felt, why it can turn up in your back or shoulder, and how to tell it apart from other kinds of pain.

When to get help straight away

Get urgent advice from your GP or NHS 111 if tummy pain lasts longer than 30 minutes. Call 999 or go to A&E if you have:

  • sudden, severe tummy pain
  • pain spreading to your back, with vomiting
  • a very high temperature, or you feel hot, cold or shivery
  • yellow skin or yellowing in the whites of your eyes

Don't drive yourself. No food or drink on this site replaces medical care.

The short answer

Gallbladder pain is usually felt in one of these places:

  • Just under your ribs on the right side. This is the most common spot.
  • In the middle of your upper tummy, just below where your ribs meet.
  • In your back, often around or just below your right shoulder blade.
  • In your right shoulder.

You might feel it in just one of these places, or in a few at once. It often starts at the front and then spreads round to the back.

Where is your gallbladder?

Before we talk about pain, it helps to know where your gallbladder actually is. It's a small pouch, about the size of a small pear. It sits on the right side of your body, tucked underneath your liver and just below your lower ribs.

Its job is simple. Your liver makes a green-yellow liquid called bile, which helps you break down fat. Your gallbladder stores the bile and squeezes it out into your gut when you eat. That's why fatty meals can set off gallbladder pain: they make the gallbladder squeeze hard.

If you put your hand flat on the right side of your tummy, just under your ribs, your gallbladder is roughly underneath your fingers. That's why most people feel the pain there.

Pain under the right ribs

This is the classic gallbladder spot. The pain is usually:

  • Sudden. It comes on fast, often 30 minutes to an hour after a meal, or in the night.
  • Strong and steady. It doesn't come and go in waves like a cramp. It builds up and stays.
  • Hard to get comfortable with. Changing position, passing wind or going to the toilet doesn't usually help.

A normal attack usually eases off within a few hours. If the area stays very sore when you press it, or it hurts more when you breathe in deeply, your gallbladder might be inflamed. That needs a doctor. Read more about the signs of an inflamed gallbladder.

Pain in the middle of your upper tummy

Lots of people don't feel gallbladder pain on the right at all. Instead they feel it in the middle of the upper tummy, right below the breastbone. This area is sometimes called the "pit of the stomach".

Because this is also where you'd feel indigestion or heartburn, gallbladder pain here is easy to mistake for a bad stomach. The difference is usually how strong it is. Gallbladder pain is often much more severe than indigestion, it lasts longer, and antacids don't help.

Pain in your back

Gallbladder pain often spreads round to your back. People describe it as:

  • a pain just below the right shoulder blade
  • a pain between the shoulder blades
  • a band of pain that seems to go right through from front to back

Back pain from your gallbladder usually comes with tummy pain at the same time, and it usually starts after eating or at night. A pulled muscle in your back is different. It normally hurts more when you move, twist or bend, and you can often point to one sore spot.

One important warning: if you have severe tummy pain that spreads to your back and you're being sick, call 999 or go to A&E. This can be a sign of an inflamed pancreas (pancreatitis), which can be caused by a gallstone.

Pain in your right shoulder

It sounds strange, but gallbladder pain can show up in your right shoulder or the tip of your shoulder. Doctors call this referred pain.

Here's what's going on. The nerves around your gallbladder share some of their "wiring" with nerves that come from your shoulder area. When the gallbladder sends a pain signal, your brain can get confused about where it's coming from, and you feel it in your shoulder instead of, or as well as, your tummy.

So if you've got right shoulder pain that comes on after a meal, alongside a pain under your ribs, your gallbladder could be the reason.

Can gallbladder pain be on the left side?

This is one of the most searched questions about gallbladder pain in the UK, so let's be clear.

Your gallbladder is on the right. So gallbladder pain is usually on the right, or in the middle. Because the middle of your upper tummy is close to the left side, some people do feel it slightly to the left. But if your pain is mainly on the left, something else is more likely to be causing it.

That doesn't mean you should ignore it. It means you should get it checked by your GP, so they can find the real cause.

Can gallbladder pain feel like chest pain?

Gallbladder pain can be felt high up, near the bottom of your chest. That can be scary, because chest pain can also be a sign of a heart problem.

Never guess with chest pain. Call 999 straight away if you have:

  • pain or tightness in your chest
  • pain that spreads to your arm, neck, jaw or back
  • shortness of breath, sweating or feeling sick with the chest pain

It's always better to be checked and told it's your gallbladder than to stay at home with a heart attack.

Is it my gallbladder or something else?

Pain in the upper tummy can have lots of causes. Only a doctor can tell you for sure, usually with a quick ultrasound scan. But here's a rough guide to how gallbladder pain compares with other common causes:

What it might beWhat it often feels like
GallbladderStrong, steady pain under the right ribs or upper middle of the tummy. Often after a fatty meal or at night. Can spread to the back or right shoulder. Lasts from around 30 minutes to a few hours.
Indigestion or heartburnA burning feeling behind the breastbone or in the upper tummy. Usually milder. Often gets better with antacids.
Trapped windCrampy pain that moves around. Usually eases when you pass wind or go to the toilet.
Pulled muscleHurts more when you move, twist or press on one spot. Not linked to eating.
Kidney stoneSevere pain in the side or back, often moving down towards the groin. Can come in waves.
Heart problemChest pain or tightness, possibly spreading to the arm, neck or jaw. Call 999.

This table is just to help you describe your pain to a doctor. It's not a way to diagnose yourself.

When does gallbladder pain usually happen?

Where the pain is gives you one clue. When it happens gives you another. Gallbladder pain often:

  • starts 30 minutes to an hour after eating, especially after a big or fatty meal
  • wakes you up in the night, often after a large evening meal
  • comes in attacks, with weeks or even months between them

If your pain follows this pattern, write it down. Note where it hurt, how long it lasted and what you'd eaten. Your GP will find this really useful. For more on the pattern, read our guide to gallbladder attacks.

What to tell your GP

Being clear about where your pain is helps your GP work out what's going on faster. Try to tell them:

  1. Where it started, and where it spread to (point to the spots)
  2. What it felt like: sharp, dull, burning, crampy or steady
  3. How long it lasted
  4. What you'd eaten before it started
  5. Anything else you noticed, like feeling sick, sweating, a high temperature or yellow skin

If your GP thinks it's your gallbladder, you'll usually be sent for an ultrasound scan. It's quick, it doesn't hurt, and it's the best way to see gallstones.

What you can do right now

Wherever you feel it, gallbladder pain usually has the same trigger: a meal that makes your gallbladder squeeze too hard. That means the food on your plate is the one thing you can change today. Small, regular meals with less fat in each one make attacks less likely for most people. Let's start with one food that's behind a lot of attacks.

Questions people ask

Where is gallbladder pain felt?

Most people feel it just under the ribs on the right side of their tummy, or in the middle of the upper tummy, just below where the ribs meet. It can spread round to the back and up to the right shoulder blade.

Where is the gallbladder located?

Your gallbladder sits on the right side of your body, tucked under your liver and just below your lower ribs. It's a small pouch about the size of a small pear.

Can gallbladder pain be felt in the back?

Yes. The pain often spreads to the back, especially to the area around or just below the right shoulder blade. Some people feel it between the shoulder blades.

Can gallbladder pain be on the left side?

It's not common. Because the pain can sit in the middle of the upper tummy, some people feel it slightly to the left. But pain that's mainly on the left side usually has a different cause, so ask your GP to check it.

Can gallbladder pain feel like chest pain?

It can be felt high up, close to the bottom of the chest. If you have chest pain, pain spreading to your arm, neck or jaw, or you feel short of breath, call 999 straight away, as these can be signs of a heart attack.

Why does gallbladder pain go to the shoulder?

This is called referred pain. Nerves from the area around your gallbladder share pathways with nerves from your shoulder, so your brain can get mixed up about where the pain is really coming from.

Is gallbladder pain the same for women and men?

Yes. It's felt in the same places. Women are more likely to get gallstones, though, so it's worth knowing the signs.

One food to avoid

Bacon and sausages

A bacon sandwich or a couple of sausages might not feel like a big meal, but they're packed with fat, and they're usually fried too. That's a double hit for your gallbladder. Try lean ham, grilled skinless chicken or turkey instead, and save the full English for a very rare treat.
