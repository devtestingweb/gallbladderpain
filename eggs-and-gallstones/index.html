Eggs and Gallstones: Can You Eat Eggs with Gallstones?

By The GallbladderPain.org editorial teamUpdated 3 November 2026

Eggs get mixed reviews in gallstone circles. Some people eat them every day without a twinge; others swear a fried egg sets them off. Both can be true, and the reason comes down to one part of the egg.

Amber: whole eggs · Green: egg whites
Yes, most people can eat eggs. Egg whites are almost fat-free; keep whole eggs to one at a time.

It's all about the yolk

A large egg has about 5 grams of fat, and almost every bit of it is in the yolk. The white is nearly pure protein with practically no fat at all.

That matters because fat is what makes your gallbladder squeeze. One yolk on its own is a modest amount of fat. Two or three fried eggs, with butter on the toast and a sausage on the side, is a big fatty meal, and that's when trouble starts.

Eggs are also on the protein list in NHS hospital dietary advice for gallstones. They're cheap, filling and quick to cook, so it's worth finding the version that suits you rather than cutting them out.

How you cook them changes everything

The same egg can be a light meal or a heavy one depending on the pan.

Easy egg swaps

  • Two-and-one omelette: one whole egg plus two whites gives you a big, satisfying omelette with the fat of a single egg.
  • Cartons of liquid egg white from the supermarket chiller make egg-white omelettes and scrambles quick.
  • Poached eggs on wholemeal toast, with no butter, instead of a fry-up.
  • Egg-white "mayo" sandwiches: chopped boiled egg white mixed with fat-free yoghurt, chives and black pepper.
  • Use a non-stick pan or a few sprays of oil, rather than a knob of butter.

What about cholesterol?

Most gallstones in the UK are made mainly of cholesterol, so it's natural to wonder whether cholesterol-rich egg yolks are a problem. The picture is less simple than it sounds. Your liver makes most of the cholesterol in your bile, and studies looking at egg-eating and new gallstones haven't given a clear answer. Things like your weight, how much fibre you eat and your meal pattern have a stronger effect.

The practical advice stays the same: enjoy eggs in modest amounts, and lean on the whites when you want more protein.

Are eggs a common trigger?

Some people find that eggs, especially fried eggs or rich yolks, bring on discomfort, even when other fatty foods don't. Everyone's tolerance is different. If you suspect eggs, try a week of egg whites only, then bring back one boiled whole egg and see how you feel. A simple food diary makes the pattern much easier to spot.

The bottom line

You don't need to give up eggs. Egg whites are a green-light food you can eat freely. Whole eggs are fine for most people one at a time, boiled or poached. Save fried eggs and creamy scrambled eggs for occasional treats, and stick to whites on bad days.

When to get help straight away

Get urgent advice from your GP or NHS 111 if tummy pain lasts longer than 30 minutes. Call 999 or go to A&E if you have:

  • sudden, severe tummy pain
  • pain spreading to your back, with vomiting
  • a very high temperature, or you feel hot, cold or shivery
  • yellow skin or yellowing in the whites of your eyes

Don't drive yourself. No food or drink on this site replaces medical care.

Questions people ask

Are eggs bad for gallstones?

Not usually. Egg whites are almost fat-free and are a great protein for people with gallstones. Whole eggs are fine for many people in sensible amounts, cooked without extra fat. The yolk holds nearly all the fat, so it's the part that may bother some people.

How many eggs can I eat a day with gallstones?

Most people do well with one whole egg at a time, or one whole egg plus extra whites if you want a bigger portion. Avoid big plates of eggs, and keep fried eggs for occasional treats.

Can I eat eggs during a gallbladder flare-up?

Stick to egg whites during a flare-up, or skip eggs until you feel better. When you bring whole eggs back, start with one boiled or poached egg and see how you feel.

Are boiled eggs OK with gallstones?

Yes. Boiling and poaching add no extra fat, so they're the best ways to cook eggs. Fried eggs, scrambled eggs made with butter or cream, and quiche are higher in fat.

Does cholesterol in eggs cause gallstones?

Most gallstones are made mainly of cholesterol, so it's a fair question. But most of the cholesterol in your bile is made by your own liver, and the evidence linking egg-eating to new gallstones is mixed. Overall weight, fibre intake and meal pattern matter more.

Is mayonnaise OK with gallstones?

Regular mayonnaise is mostly oil and egg yolk, so it's high in fat. Use light mayonnaise sparingly, or swap in fat-free yoghurt with a squeeze of lemon.
