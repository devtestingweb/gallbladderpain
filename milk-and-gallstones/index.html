Milk and Gallstones: Which Milk Is Best for Your Gallbladder?

By The GallbladderPain.org editorial teamUpdated 3 November 2026

Milk is in almost everything: your morning tea, your cereal, your coffee, your sauces. If you have gallstones, the type of milk you buy can quietly make a big difference to how much fat you eat every day.

How much fat is in your milk?

The difference between milks is bigger than most people think. A glass of whole milk has around twenty times the fat of the same glass of skimmed.

Why the type of milk matters

Fat is what makes your gallbladder squeeze, and the squeeze is what causes pain when a stone is in the way. One splash of whole milk in your tea won't do much. But milk is something most of us have several times a day, so the fat adds up: two milky coffees, a bowl of cereal and a white sauce at dinner, all made with whole milk, is a fair amount of dairy fat before you've counted anything else.

Switching to skimmed or 1% is one of the easiest changes you can make. You keep the protein and calcium, and lose almost all the fat.

What about plant milks?

Most unsweetened plant milks in cartons are low in fat, and many are fortified with calcium. Here's how they compare:

  • Almond milk (unsweetened): about 1g of fat per 100ml. A light option.
  • Oat milk: about 1.5g per 100ml for standard versions. "Barista" versions often have added oil and can be closer to 3g, so check the label.
  • Soya milk (unsweetened): about 2g per 100ml, with more protein than other plant milks.
  • Coconut milk drink (carton): about 1g per 100ml. Very different from tinned coconut milk, which is high in fat.

Choose versions with added calcium, and avoid sweetened or "creamy" versions if you're watching fat.

Is milk OK during a flare-up?

Many people tolerate skimmed milk well on bad days, for example on porridge or in a cup of tea. Avoid whole milk, cream, milkshakes and hot chocolate until you feel better. If milk seems to upset you even when it's skimmed, it may be worth asking your GP about lactose intolerance, which can cause similar bloating and tummy pain.

The bottom line

You don't need to give up milk. Swap whole milk for skimmed or 1%, ask for skimmed in coffee shops, and keep milkshakes and creamy hot chocolates as rare treats. It's one of the simplest changes you can make, and you'll barely notice it after a week or two.

When to get help straight away

Get urgent advice from your GP or NHS 111 if tummy pain lasts longer than 30 minutes. Call 999 or go to A&E if you have:

  • sudden, severe tummy pain
  • pain spreading to your back, with vomiting
  • a very high temperature, or you feel hot, cold or shivery
  • yellow skin or yellowing in the whites of your eyes

Don't drive yourself. No food or drink on this site replaces medical care.

Questions people ask

Is milk bad for gallstones?

Whole milk is higher in fat, so it's best limited, especially in large amounts. Skimmed and 1% milk are low in fat and suit most people with gallstones well.

Which milk is best for gallstones?

Skimmed milk has the least fat, with almost none per glass. 1% milk is a good middle ground if you find skimmed too thin. Unsweetened plant milks such as almond, oat or soya are also low in fat.

Can I have milk in my tea and coffee?

Yes. A splash of semi-skimmed in a cup of tea is a very small amount of fat. It's large milky drinks made with whole milk, like a big latte or hot chocolate, that add up.

Is milk OK during a gallbladder flare-up?

Skimmed milk is usually fine in small amounts, for example on porridge. Avoid whole milk, milkshakes and creamy drinks until you feel better.

Is lactose a problem with gallstones?

Lactose doesn't affect gallstones. But lactose intolerance can cause bloating and tummy pain, which can be confused with gallbladder symptoms. If you notice symptoms with all milk, including skimmed, mention it to your GP.

Is coconut milk OK with gallstones?

It depends which kind. The coconut milk drink sold in cartons next to other plant milks is low in fat. Tinned coconut milk used in cooking is very high in fat, so swap it for reduced-fat coconut milk or a tomato-based sauce.
