Gallbladder Symptoms: 10 Signs of a Gallbladder Problem

By The GallbladderPain.org editorial teamUpdated 3 November 2026

If you've had a pain under your ribs that stopped you in your tracks, you'll want to know if it's your gallbladder. Here are the signs doctors look for, which ones are an emergency, and what you can do about it.

When to get help straight away

Get urgent advice from your GP or NHS 111 if tummy pain lasts longer than 30 minutes. Call 999 or go to A&E if you have:

  • sudden, severe tummy pain
  • pain spreading to your back, with vomiting
  • a very high temperature, or you feel hot, cold or shivery
  • yellow skin or yellowing in the whites of your eyes

Don't drive yourself. No food or drink on this site replaces medical care.

Most gallstones cause no symptoms at all

It's worth knowing this first. Most people with gallstones never feel a thing, and many only find out by chance during a scan for something else. Symptoms usually start when a stone blocks the narrow tube that drains your gallbladder. That's when the signs below appear.

The 10 signs of a gallbladder problem

1. Sudden, severe pain under the right ribs

The classic sign. The pain is usually just under your ribs on the right-hand side, or in the middle of your upper tummy below the breastbone. It tends to build quickly and stay severe and steady, rather than coming and going.

2. Pain spreading to your back or right shoulder blade

Gallbladder pain often travels round to your back or up to the right shoulder blade. Many people are surprised by this and think they've pulled a muscle.

3. Pain after eating, especially a fatty meal

Attacks often start 30 minutes to an hour after a meal. A fatty meal makes the gallbladder squeeze hard, and if a stone is in the way, that squeeze hurts.

4. Pain that wakes you at night

Many attacks happen in the evening or wake people from sleep, often after a large evening meal.

5. Feeling sick or being sick

Nausea and vomiting are common during an attack. Unlike some stomach upsets, being sick doesn't usually ease the pain.

6. Sweating during the pain

Many people break out in a sweat when an attack is at its worst.

7. Pain that won't settle, with tenderness under the ribs

A normal attack eases within a few hours. If the pain lasts longer, the area under your right ribs is very tender, and it hurts more when you breathe in deeply, your gallbladder may be inflamed. This is called cholecystitis and needs treatment.

8. A high temperature or shivering

A fever, or feeling hot and cold and shivery, alongside tummy pain can mean an infection. Get help straight away.

9. Yellow skin or eyes, and itchy skin

If a stone blocks the main bile duct, bile backs up into your blood. This causes jaundice: yellowing of the skin or the whites of the eyes. It can be harder to spot on brown or black skin, so check the whites of the eyes. Itchy skin often comes with it.

10. Dark pee and pale poo

Darker urine and paler stools than usual are another sign that bile isn't flowing properly. Together with yellowing, they mean you need medical advice urgently.

What about bloating, burping and indigestion?

Lots of people with gallstones say they feel bloated, burp a lot or get indigestion, especially after rich food. It's real, but these symptoms are so common in everyone that on their own they can't tell you it's your gallbladder. The pain is the giveaway. If you only have bloating and indigestion, your GP may look at other causes first.

How gallbladder problems are diagnosed

If your GP thinks it's your gallbladder, you'll usually have an ultrasound scan of your tummy. It's quick, painless and the best way to see gallstones. Blood tests can check your liver and look for signs of infection. If the stones are causing symptoms, you'll be offered treatment, which is most often keyhole surgery to remove the gallbladder.

What you can do right now

While you wait for tests or treatment, the biggest thing in your control is what's on your plate. Large, fatty meals are the most common trigger for attacks. Eating smaller meals, more regularly, and cutting back on the worst trigger foods makes a real difference for most people. So let's start with one.

Questions people ask

What are the first signs of gallbladder problems?

For most people the first sign is a sudden, severe pain under the ribs on the right or in the middle of the upper tummy, often after a meal or at night. Many people have gallstones for years without any symptoms at all.

Can you have gallstones without symptoms?

Yes. Most people with gallstones never have symptoms, and the stones are often found by chance on a scan for something else. Treatment usually isn't needed unless they start causing problems.

Do gallstones cause bloating and burping?

Many people with gallstones report bloating, burping and indigestion, but these symptoms are very common and have many other causes, so on their own they don't point to the gallbladder. Severe pain under the right ribs is the more typical sign.

How do doctors check for gallstones?

Usually with an ultrasound scan of your tummy, which is quick and painless. You may also have blood tests to check your liver and look for infection.

Can gallbladder problems cause pain on the left side?

Gallbladder pain is usually on the right or in the middle of the upper tummy. Pain mainly on the left side is more likely to have another cause, so it's worth getting it checked by your GP.

Can gallstones go away on their own?

Small stones sometimes pass out of the gallbladder by themselves, but most gallstones stay put. If they're causing symptoms, the usual long-term treatment is keyhole surgery to remove the gallbladder.

One food to avoid

Fried food

Chips, fried chicken, battered fish and the full English are the number one trigger people report. Frying soaks food in oil, and a big hit of fat is exactly what makes a gallbladder with stones in it squeeze hard. The good news: you can keep the crunch by oven-baking or air-frying with a teaspoon of oil instead.
