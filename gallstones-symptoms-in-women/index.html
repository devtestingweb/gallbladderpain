Gallstones Symptoms in Women: Signs, Pain Location and Pregnancy

By The GallbladderPain.org editorial teamUpdated 3 November 2026

Gallstones are far more common in women than in men, and many women put up with the pain for months before getting answers. Here's what the symptoms look like, why women are more at risk, and what you can do to help yourself.

When to get help straight away

Get urgent advice from your GP or NHS 111 if tummy pain lasts longer than 30 minutes. Call 999 or go to A&E if you have:

  • sudden, severe tummy pain
  • pain spreading to your back, with vomiting
  • a very high temperature, or you feel hot, cold or shivery
  • yellow skin or yellowing in the whites of your eyes

Don't drive yourself. No food or drink on this site replaces medical care.

Women are about twice as likely to get gallstones

Being a woman is one of the main risk factors for gallstones, alongside being over 40 and carrying extra weight. Women are roughly twice as likely as men to develop them, especially during the years they can have children. After the menopause, the gap narrows.

The main reason is the hormone oestrogen. It raises the amount of cholesterol in your bile, and most gallstones in the UK are made mainly of cholesterol. Progesterone also slows down how well the gallbladder empties, so bile sits for longer. That's why anything that raises these hormones is linked to gallstones.

Don't stop the pill or HRT because of this. The increase is small for most women, and your GP can help you weigh it up.

Gallbladder symptoms in women

The symptoms are the same as in men. What's different is how often they're put down to something else, like indigestion, IBS or a back problem. The signs to look for are:

  • Sudden, severe pain under the ribs on the right, or in the middle of the upper tummy
  • Pain spreading to your back or right shoulder blade
  • Pain after a meal, especially a fatty one, or pain that wakes you at night
  • Feeling sick or being sick, and sweating with the pain
  • Pain lasting from around 30 minutes to a few hours, then easing off

For the full list, including the signs of something more serious, read our guide to the 10 signs of a gallbladder problem.

Where is gallbladder pain felt in women?

Exactly where it's felt in men. Your gallbladder sits under your liver on the right-hand side, so the pain is usually under the right ribs or in the middle of your upper tummy, and it can spread to your back and right shoulder blade.

What about the left side? Lots of women search for this. Because the pain can sit in the middle of the upper tummy, some people feel it more towards the left. But pain mainly on the left side is more likely to have another cause, so ask your GP to check it.

Gallstones in pregnancy

Pregnancy hormones make bile thicker and slow down the gallbladder, so gallstones and "sludge" are more common when you're pregnant. Many cases cause no symptoms, and sludge often clears after the birth.

If you have tummy pain while pregnant, contact your midwife or GP. Your team will talk you through the options, which often start with changes to what you eat and pain relief that's safe in pregnancy.

Itching in pregnancy is a different condition

Very itchy skin in pregnancy, especially on the palms of the hands or soles of the feet, or itching that's worse at night, can be a sign of a liver condition called intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (ICP). It isn't caused by gallstones, but it needs checking quickly. The NHS advises calling your maternity unit straight away, or NHS 111 if you can't reach them.

Why women's gallbladder pain gets missed

Many women are told it's indigestion, stress or a pulled muscle, sometimes for months. If your pain matches the pattern above, and especially if it follows meals or wakes you at night, tell your GP exactly that: where it hurts, how long it lasts, and what you'd eaten. A simple ultrasound scan is usually all it takes to find gallstones.

Questions people ask

Are gallstone symptoms different in women?

The symptoms themselves are the same in women and men: pain under the right ribs or in the upper middle of the tummy, often spreading to the back or right shoulder blade, sometimes with sickness. Women are simply more likely to get gallstones in the first place.

Why are women more likely to get gallstones?

Mainly because of the hormone oestrogen, which raises the amount of cholesterol in bile. That's why pregnancy, the combined pill and HRT are all linked to a higher risk. Women are roughly twice as likely as men to develop gallstones, and the gap narrows after the menopause.

Where is gallbladder pain felt in a woman?

In the same place as in men: under the ribs on the right-hand side or in the middle of the upper tummy. It can spread to the back and the right shoulder blade.

Can gallbladder pain be on the left side in women?

It's unusual. Gallbladder pain can be felt in the middle of the upper tummy, which some people describe as more to the left, but pain mainly on the left side usually has another cause. See your GP to get it checked.

Are gallstones common in pregnancy?

Pregnancy hormones make gallstones and biliary sludge (thickened bile) more likely. Many cases cause no symptoms and settle after the birth. If you have tummy pain in pregnancy, contact your midwife or GP.

Can the contraceptive pill cause gallstones?

Pills containing oestrogen have been linked to a small increase in gallstone risk, mainly in women who are already prone to them. Don't stop your contraception because of this; talk to your GP or pharmacist if you're worried.

One food to avoid

Whole milk

Milk goes into tea, coffee, cereal and sauces several times a day, so the type you buy really adds up. A glass of whole milk has around 7g of fat; the same glass of skimmed has less than half a gram. Switching to skimmed or 1% is one of the easiest changes you can make, and you keep all the calcium, which matters for women's bones.
