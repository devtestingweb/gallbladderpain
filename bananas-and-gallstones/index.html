Bananas and Gallstones: Are Bananas Good for Your Gallbladder?

By The GallbladderPain.org editorial teamUpdated 3 November 2026

If you have gallstones and want a snack you never need to worry about, reach for a banana. They're one of the safest foods on the whole list, and they're on our gentle "flare-day" list too.

Green: choose often
Yes, bananas are one of the safest fruits you can eat with gallstones.

Why bananas are so gallbladder-friendly

The main thing that sets off gallstone pain is a large, fatty meal. When fat reaches your gut, your gallbladder squeezes out bile to help digest it, and if a stone is in the way, that squeeze hurts. A medium banana has well under half a gram of fat, so it asks almost nothing of your gallbladder.

Bananas bring three other things to the table:

  • Gentle fibre. About 3g per banana, much of it soluble fibre, which is kind to a sensitive gut. A high-fibre diet is part of the standard NHS advice for gallstones.
  • Easy digestion. A ripe banana is soft, mild and filling. That's why it's a staple of bland diets for upset stomachs.
  • Potassium. Useful if you've been sick and need to top up what you've lost.

Ripe or green: does it matter?

For most people, a ripe banana (yellow, with a few brown freckles) is the better choice when your gallbladder is playing up. As a banana ripens, its starch turns to natural sugar and it gets softer and easier to digest.

Greener bananas contain more resistant starch. That's good for gut bacteria, but it can cause wind and bloating in some people. If you're already uncomfortable, that's the last thing you need. Save green bananas for good days.

The best ways to eat bananas with gallstones

  • On its own, as a snack between meals. Eating little and often keeps your gallbladder emptying gently.
  • Sliced on porridge made with water or skimmed milk. This is one of the best breakfasts you can have on a bad day.
  • With fat-free Greek yoghurt and a sprinkle of oats.
  • In a homemade smoothie with skimmed milk and a handful of berries.
  • Frozen and blended into "nice cream": a creamy dessert with none of the fat of real ice cream.
  • Mashed on toast instead of butter.

What about bananas and blood sugar?

Bananas contain natural sugar, so if you have diabetes (which is more common in people with gallstones), pair your banana with some protein, such as fat-free yoghurt, or choose a smaller, less ripe one. One a day fits comfortably into most diets. Ask your diabetes team if you're unsure.

The bottom line

Bananas are cheap, easy and about as gallbladder-friendly as food gets. Keep a bunch in the fruit bowl, and when you're not sure what's safe to eat, a banana is always a good answer.

When to get help straight away

Get urgent advice from your GP or NHS 111 if tummy pain lasts longer than 30 minutes. Call 999 or go to A&E if you have:

  • sudden, severe tummy pain
  • pain spreading to your back, with vomiting
  • a very high temperature, or you feel hot, cold or shivery
  • yellow skin or yellowing in the whites of your eyes

Don't drive yourself. No food or drink on this site replaces medical care.

Questions people ask

Can I eat bananas during a gallbladder attack?

During an attack many people can't face food at all, and that's fine for a short while. When you're ready to eat again, a ripe banana is one of the gentlest foods to start with. If pain lasts longer than 30 minutes, get urgent medical advice.

How many bananas a day can I eat with gallstones?

One or two a day is a sensible amount for most people. They're very low in fat, so the limit is about keeping your diet varied and your sugar intake reasonable, not about your gallbladder.

Are banana smoothies good for gallstones?

Yes, if you make them yourself with skimmed milk or fat-free yoghurt. Shop-bought smoothies and smoothie-bar drinks often add nut butter, full-fat yoghurt or ice cream, which can push the fat up a lot.

Can bananas dissolve gallstones?

No. Bananas are a great food for people with gallstones, but no food has been shown to dissolve stones. Be wary of anyone who says otherwise.

Are bananas good after gallbladder removal?

Yes. Bananas are easy to digest and their soluble fibre can help firm up loose stools, which some people get after surgery.

Is banana bread OK with gallstones?

Usually not as an everyday food. Most banana bread recipes use a lot of butter or oil, so a thick slice can carry more fat than you'd guess. Try a reduced-fat recipe, or have a small slice occasionally.
