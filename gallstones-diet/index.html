Gallstones Diet: What to Eat, What to Limit and What to Avoid

By The GallbladderPain.org editorial teamUpdated 3 November 2026

The right diet for gallstones isn't about giving up everything you enjoy. It comes down to smaller meals, less fat at any one sitting, and plenty of fibre. This guide shows you what that looks like on your plate.

The short version

  • Eat little and often. Three regular meals, plus small snacks if you need them. Don't skip breakfast.
  • Spread your fat out. One large, fatty meal is the most common trigger for an attack.
  • Grill, steam, boil, poach or microwave instead of frying or roasting in oil.
  • Aim for at least five portions of fruit and vegetables a day and choose wholegrains.
  • Drink 6 to 8 glasses of fluid a day, mostly water.
  • If you need to lose weight, go slowly, no faster than about 1kg (2lb) a week.

Do you really need a low-fat diet?

This surprises most people. NHS dietitians say a strict low-fat diet isn't needed for everyone with gallstones, and there's little evidence that cutting fat to the bone helps. Fat is part of a balanced diet, and small amounts of healthy fats like olive oil, nuts and avocado are good for you.

So why does everyone say "avoid fat"? When you eat a fatty meal, your gallbladder squeezes to push bile into your gut to help digest it. If a gallstone is in the way, that squeeze can bring on pain. The bigger and fattier the meal, the harder the squeeze.

That gives you a simple, workable rule: it's the amount of fat in one sitting that matters most. A teaspoon of olive oil on your salad is a very different thing from a full English breakfast. If you notice fatty food triggers your symptoms, cut back further. Everyone's tolerance is different.

How to spot hidden fat on food labels

UK food packs use traffic-light colours, and they're one of the easiest tools you have. Look at the fat figure per 100g:

Label colourFat per 100gWhat it means for you
Green3g or lessLow fat. A good everyday choice.
Amber3g to 17.5gMedium. Fine in sensible portions; check how much you're eating.
Redover 17.5gHigh fat. Keep portions small or save it for occasional treats.

Watch for foods that sound healthy but aren't: granola, shop-bought smoothies with nut butter, "light" ready meals with creamy sauces and coleslaw can all carry more fat than you'd expect.

When you eat matters as much as what you eat

Your gallbladder stores bile between meals and empties when you eat. Long gaps without food let bile sit and concentrate, and regular meals keep it moving. That's why the NHS advice is to eat regular meals, have breakfast soon after you get up, and avoid fasting or skipping meals.

If big meals leave you uncomfortable, switch to smaller portions more often: three modest meals and two light snacks works well for many people.

The same logic applies to weight loss. Losing weight is a good idea if you're carrying extra, but crash diets and rapid weight loss make gallstones more likely. Aim for no more than about 1kg (2lb) a week.

When your gallbladder is grumbling, keep things simple, low in fat and small. This is the sort of day that suits most people:

When to get help straight away

Get urgent advice from your GP or NHS 111 if tummy pain lasts longer than 30 minutes. Call 999 or go to A&E if you have:

  • sudden, severe tummy pain
  • pain spreading to your back, with vomiting
  • a very high temperature, or you feel hot, cold or shivery
  • yellow skin or yellowing in the whites of your eyes

Don't drive yourself. No food or drink on this site replaces medical care.

Questions people ask

Can diet get rid of gallstones?

No food or diet has been shown to dissolve gallstones that are already there. What diet can do is lower the chance of painful attacks and support your general health while you wait for treatment or decide on next steps with your doctor.

Do I need to cut out fat completely?

No. NHS dietitians say a strict low-fat diet isn't needed for everyone. Small amounts of healthy fats are part of a balanced diet. If fatty food triggers your symptoms, cut back on it, and keep portions of fat small at each meal.

What can I eat during a gallbladder flare-up?

Stick to gentle, low-fat foods in small portions: broth-based soups, porridge, rice, toast, bananas, plain potatoes, steamed vegetables, chicken breast and white fish. If pain lasts longer than 30 minutes, get urgent medical advice.

Should I lose weight if I have gallstones?

If you're carrying extra weight, losing some can help, but do it gradually. Losing more than about 1kg (2lb) a week, or crash and yo-yo dieting, can make gallstones more likely.

Do I need a special diet after gallbladder removal?

Usually not. Most people can return to a normal, balanced diet. Some find large fatty meals cause loose stools for a while. Speak to your doctor if diarrhoea doesn't settle.

Is coffee OK with gallstones?

Black coffee has almost no fat, so the coffee itself is unlikely to be the problem. Watch what comes with it: whole milk, cream, syrups and pastries add up quickly.
