# Writing rules for GallbladderPain.org

Follow these for every new article. They come straight from the site owner.

## Voice
- Write in simple, conversational English that a 14-year-old would understand.
- Short sentences. Everyday words. Explain any medical word the first time you use it, e.g. "biliary colic (the medical name for a gallbladder attack)".
- Talk to the reader as "you".
- **Never use em dashes (—) or en dashes (–).** Use a full stop, a comma, "and", "but" or brackets instead.
- UK spelling and UK services: GP, NHS 111, 999, A&E, paracetamol, "poo", "tummy".

## SEO articles (anything we want Google traffic for)
- Target one high-volume UK search term (use the Keyword Planner files). Put it in the page title, H1, first paragraph and at least one H2.
- Long form: aim for 1,800 to 2,500 words. Answer the search fully, plus the follow-up questions people ask.
- Lead with the reader's pain or worry.
- Put the "When to get help straight away" box near the top of any symptom or pain article.
- Include an FAQ of 6 to 7 real questions (these get FAQ schema automatically).
- **End with ONE food** to have or avoid (`one_food()`), then the fridge guide sign-up straight after.
- No mid-article sign-up box on these pages. The sidebar box (desktop) and floating button (phone) stay.
- Link to 3 or more related articles on the site.
- Check every medical fact against the NHS, NICE or an NHS hospital trust, and list sources at the bottom.
- Never promise a cure, a way to dissolve stones naturally, or avoiding surgery. Never mention the supplement in articles.

## Paid-traffic page (/free-guide/)
- The long-form rules do NOT apply. Keep it short and focused on one action: sign up.
- No menu, no sidebar, no other links except privacy, disclaimer and about (needed for Google Ads).
- Hidden from Google search (noindex).
